What Is Conversational Marketing, and What Changed?

Conversational marketing is how you turn website visits into sales conversations in real time. Instead of a form and a slow follow-up, your buyer asks a question and gets an answer right away. The same conversation qualifies the buyer and routes them to sales. Today an AI assistant often runs it, where a scripted chatbot once did.
VP
Vijay Satya Prakash
November 12, 2026

Forty-two hours is a long time to leave a buyer with an unanswered question. Conversational marketing exists to close that gap. This guide explains what it means, how it differs from the chat tools you may already have, and how to compare the software.

What does conversational marketing mean?

It means your website talks with buyers instead of waiting for them to fill in a form. Someone lands on your pricing page with a question. They ask it, get an answer and keep going. Nobody has to wait for a reply email.

Along the way, one conversation does three jobs a form can't:

  • It answers. The buyer gets a reply to the question they came with, on the page they're reading.
  • It qualifies. You learn whether they're a fit while they're still interested.
  • It routes. A meeting gets booked, a rep gets an alert, or a person joins when the buyer asks for one.

Drift, the chat company, coined the term. Drift and Wiley published a book called Conversational Marketing in 2019. The idea hasn't moved much since: let buyers ask, and answer them while they're still on your site.

What has moved is who does the talking. That shift explains most of the confusion around the term. First, though, it helps to see what goes wrong without it.

Why do forms and slow follow-up lose buyers?

Because the buyer's interest peaks while they're on your site, and a form makes them wait past that moment. You've probably seen the symptom already. Demo requests come in, someone replies the next afternoon, and a few of those people never write back.

The mechanism is simple. A form collects a name and an email, then hands the next step to a person on your team. That person is in meetings, in another time zone or asleep. By the time the reply goes out, the buyer has opened three other tabs.

The research on this is old, and it still stings. For a 2011 article in Harvard Business Review, researchers sent a web lead to 2,241 US companies and timed each reply. Only 37% replied within an hour, and 23% never replied at all. Among companies that replied within 30 days, the average reply took 42 hours.

The same article reported a separate analysis of 1.25 million leads received by 42 US companies. Firms that contacted a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited an hour longer. Against firms that waited 24 hours or more, the gap was more than 60 times.

Buyers have also changed what they expect. In a Gartner survey published in March 2026, 67% of 646 B2B buyers said they prefer a buying experience without a sales rep. And 45% said they had used AI during a recent purchase.

Put those together and the consequence is plain. Your buyer wants answers now, often without talking to sales, and a form gives them neither. A faster form won't fix that. Answering the question while the buyer is still asking it will.

How is conversational marketing different from older chat tools?

Older chat tools either waited for a person to type a reply or followed a script someone wrote in advance. Today's tools answer from your own content, so they can handle the question nobody planned for. That's the plain difference, and it explains the "chatbot vs conversational AI" debate you'll see elsewhere.

It helps to look at the four tools teams have used, in roughly the order they arrived.

Web forms. The form is still on most B2B sites. It captures contact details well, but it can't answer anything, and the follow-up depends on how busy your team is that day.

Live chat. Live chat put a real person in the corner of the page. That works beautifully when someone is online. Outside staffed hours, the visitor gets an away message and a form.

Scripted chatbots. This is where conversational marketing grew up. Most chatbots of that era ran on decision trees: click a button, get the next prepared message. A good script routes the common questions fast. An unexpected question breaks it, and the visitor usually ends up back at a form.

AI assistants. An AI assistant reads the question, looks up the answer in the pages and documents you gave it, and replies in plain language. That lookup step is called retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG for short. It means the reply is built from your material rather than from a script or the open web.

The clearest sign of the shift is what happened to Drift itself. Salesloft acquired Drift in February 2024. In March 2026, the combined Clari + Salesloft announced a gradual sunset of Drift and began referring its customers to 1mind. Salesloft's chat page now says it has "transitioned from Drift to 1mind." The company that named the category is moving its own customers to AI.

So when someone says "conversational marketing" today, ask which kind of conversation they mean. A script and an AI assistant both chat, but only one of them can answer a question it hasn't seen before.

What does an AI assistant do in one conversation?

It answers the buyer's questions, works out how good a fit they are, and decides what should happen next. All three happen in the same chat, while the buyer is still on your page.

Here's the general shape, whichever tool you use:

  1. Answer. The assistant looks up the buyer's question in your content and replies. If your content doesn't cover it, a good assistant says so instead of guessing.
  2. Qualify. As the chat goes on, it picks up signals about fit and intent. Many teams use BANT for this: budget, authority, need and timeline.
  3. Route. When a buyer looks ready, the assistant offers a meeting, alerts your team, or lets the buyer reach a person.

How do you run conversational marketing on a B2B website?

Start on the pages where buyers already show intent, and widen from there. You don't need a new process on day one. You need answers in the right places and a clear rule for what counts as a good lead.

  1. Pick your high-intent pages first. Pricing, demo, product and comparison pages are where buyers arrive with real questions. An assistant on your careers page won't teach you much.
  2. Give the assistant your best content. Product pages, docs, case studies and your pricing FAQ are the raw material for every answer. If a question keeps coming up and the content is thin, the assistant will be thin too.
  3. Decide what qualified means. Write down the two or three signals that make a lead worth a rep's time. Our BANT guide and lead qualification guide walk through how to choose them.
  4. Set who hears about a good lead, and how fast. A qualified lead that sits in an inbox overnight brings back the 42-hour problem from earlier. Name the people who get alerts and the channel they already watch.
  5. Let ready buyers book, and let anyone reach a person. Some buyers want a meeting now. Others want to ask a person one more question first. Give both a way through.
  6. Read the questions it couldn't answer. Every unanswered question points to a gap in your content. In ZipTier, the Knowledge Base Gaps panel is documented to flag these. Fill the gaps each week, and the answers improve without anyone rewriting a script.

That's conversational AI for lead generation in practice: answer where intent is high, qualify inside the chat, and get ready buyers to your team fast.

How do you compare conversational marketing software?

Judge each tool on how it answers, how it qualifies and how it hands off, then check the pricing unit.

FAQs

Is conversational marketing the same as a chatbot?

No. A chatbot is one tool you might use, while conversational marketing is the approach behind it. The approach is to answer, qualify and route buyers in a live conversation, whether a script, a person or an AI assistant does the talking.

Is conversational marketing the same as live chat?

No. Live chat is one channel, staffed by people on your team. Conversational marketing can run on live chat, an AI assistant or both, with a person joining when the buyer asks.

What is a chat marketing platform?

It's software that runs conversations on your website, captures and qualifies leads, and passes them to sales or your CRM.

How is conversational sales different from conversational marketing?

The two overlap almost completely on a B2B website. Conversational marketing usually describes the whole approach, from the first question to the handoff. Conversational sales puts the weight on the later part, where a qualified buyer books a meeting or talks to a rep.

What happened to Drift?

Salesloft acquired Drift in February 2024. In March 2026, Clari + Salesloft announced a gradual sunset of Drift and began referring its customers to 1mind. Salesloft hasn't published a shutdown date on its own pages.
